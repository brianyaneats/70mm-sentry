How it works

What Dropwatch does, and what it doesn't

This page explains, plainly, what happens between a drop opening and an alert reaching you — and where our responsibility ends and yours begins.

What this is

Dropwatch watches scarce-inventory drops — a campsite reservation freed up by someone else's cancellation, a movie finally going on sale — and alerts you the moment something opens.

An alert from us is a head start, not a guarantee. Everyone else watching that same drop is racing for the same inventory, and getting our alert doesn't put you first in line. It just means you're not finding out an hour late.

Dropwatch never buys, reserves, or holds anything on your behalf. We tell you; what you do with that is entirely up to you.

How the watching works

Each drop is checked on its own clock. In steady state, that might mean once a minute. Inside a window where we know something is likely to happen — a scheduled release time, a pattern we've seen before — the checks tighten up to every few seconds.

Those checks are deliberately paced to stay well inside what each source can comfortably handle. We'd rather be a few seconds slower than be the reason a source gets locked down for everyone. One check covers every subscriber watching that drop — ten people watching or ten thousand, the source sees the same, single check either way.

What we promise (and measure)

We're early — these are targets, not a track record yet. Once we have production history, these numbers will be verified continuously against it. Until then, the figures below come from pre-production load tests on developer hardware, not live traffic.

  • A notification is dispatched within 60 seconds of detection. During early access everyone is on the free tier — there is nothing to buy yet. The paid tier, when it exists, will get alerts immediately while free stays up to 60 seconds behind, by design.
  • In testing, our internal delivery pipeline handled 10,000 notifications with zero lost.
  • If nobody receives an alert within 5 minutes of an event we detected, that pages a human. Silence is treated as an outage, not as luck.
  • Every alert either confirms it was delivered or retries. Nothing is fire-and-forget.

What we don't promise: that you'll win the inventory — that's between you and everyone else watching — or that an unofficial data source stays available. Some sources aren't official APIs; they can change or go dark without notice. When that risk applies to a drop, we say so on the card itself, not in fine print:

Fragile source

We never sell a paid plan on one of those drops. If we can't promise a source will still be there next month, we shouldn't be taking your money for it — so the code refuses that combination outright, rather than leaving it to our judgment.

How each drop is vetted

Every drop is sourced from official or public data only. If watching something would require defeating a bot wall or logging into someone's private account, we refuse it at intake — permanently, not as a "maybe later."

Before anyone can subscribe, every new drop runs for a period in shadow mode: we watch the real source ourselves, with no subscribers attached, to make sure it behaves the way we expect before we ask anyone to trust it.

Subscribing

Three steps, whichever channel you pick.

Discord

  1. Create a webhook for the channel you want alerts in.
  2. Paste that webhook URL into Dropwatch.
  3. You're subscribed. Alerts post straight to that channel.

ntfy

  1. Pick a topic name in the ntfy phone app.
  2. Enter that same topic in Dropwatch.
  3. You're subscribed. Alerts arrive as phone notifications.

Your own webhook

  1. Enter a URL you control.
  2. Read the confirmation code we deliver to it.
  3. You're subscribed. See Automating on your side.

Telegram is on the way. We're finishing the setup step that lets the bot message you safely, and we'd rather ship it late than offer a sign-up that can't finish.

SMS isn't offered yet. Carrier compliance for text messaging takes real time to do right, and we'd rather wait than cut corners on it.

Automating on your side

Every alert can also be delivered as a signed, machine-readable event to a URL you control — a webhook. That lets your own scripts, running on your own machines with your own accounts, react the instant a drop opens.

We never ask for or store your credentials for any booking site. Dropwatch's job is telling you fast; what happens after that is yours by design.

Automate responsibly. Some venues prohibit automated purchasing, and we will never help you circumvent that.
